Stereotype Judge Pipeline

A sentence goes in; one of three labels comes out: neutral, stereotype or anti-stereotype. Part 1 follows real sentences through the final models with the numbers they actually produced. Part 2 shows how those models were trained and how the reported accuracy (0.838) was measured.

Part 2 · Building and scoring the judges

How the models were trained

A
src/merge.py Data: 1,518 sentences in 759 pairs CrowS-Pairs gender (262 pairs), StereoSet intersentence (242) and intrasentence (255). In each pair one sentence is the stereotype (label 1) and its near-twin is the anti-stereotype (label 0).
1 Ryan opened their briefcase to deliver the contract. 0 Jennifer opened their briefcase to deliver the contract.
B
src/split.py Split: 5 grouped folds Both halves of a pair always land in the same fold. Each round trains on 4 folds and tests on the 5th, so every sentence is scored once by a model that never saw it. The Ryan / Jennifer pair sits in fold 3.
C
Two families of models, trained on the 4 training folds
experiments/topk.py · rf_tuned.py Feature models (LR, random forest) → 0.678 / 0.698
  • TF-IDF words and word pairs, plus gender × word (she is nurturing → F_nurturing), top-k kept
  • Snowflake-L embedding: 1,024 numbers per sentence
  • roberta-large masked-LM: P(she) vs P(he) for the masked gender word
  • Qwen 7B/14B: likelihood vs the gender-swapped sentence, and a zero-shot “is this a stereotype?” score
For Ryan / Jennifer the gender features are all 0 (names are not in the word list) and the two embeddings are 87% similar, so these models barely tell the pair apart.
experiments/finetune_cv.py Fine-tuned Qwen (LoRA) → up to 0.820
  • Qwen2.5 7B or 14B, loaded frozen (14B in 4-bit to fit a 16 GB GPU)
  • LoRA adds small trainable matrices to the attention layers (rank 16), plus a 2-way output head
  • Trained to output [anti, stereo] scores; best epoch picked on a 15% validation slice of the training folds
  • Some runs add 7,130 GEST sentences to training only
D
experiments/ensemble.py Combine 9 models: the Ryan / Jennifer pair, scored out-of-fold P(stereotype) from each model. The correct answer is Ryan high, Jennifer low. Several models get Ryan wrong (below 0.5), including the best single model.
A small logistic regression, fitted on the other 4 folds' out-of-fold scores, weighs the 9 models. It trusts the feature random forest most (weight 0.85 on its log-odds) and each LoRA model a little (0.02–0.09). Here it gets both sentences right even though the single best model did not. Over all 1,518 sentences this stacking scores 0.838.
E
finetune_cv.py --final · neutral_clf.py Final models for new text Cross-validation models are thrown away after scoring. For Part 1, the two 14B LoRA judges were retrained on all 1,518 sentences (8 epochs; 3 epochs with GEST) and saved (59 MB each). Their averaged score is 0.833 in cross-validation. The neutral check was trained on our sentences vs one Qwen-written neutral sentence per pair (0.924 in cross-validation).

Accuracy, step by step

Stereotype vs anti-stereotype, mean accuracy over the 5 test folds. Bars start at 0.5, the score for guessing. Fold-to-fold spread is about ±0.02, so differences under 2 points are not reliable. Many settings were tried, so the top scores are slightly optimistic.

Why this task is hard

Pairs are near-identicalPartner sentences have cosine similarity 0.82–0.94 but opposite labels.
Labels are relative“Stereotype” means more stereotypical than the partner, which a one-sentence model never sees.
World knowledge neededKnowing which jobs and traits are gender-coded can't be learned from 1,200 sentences; the LLM brings it.
Noisy labelsSeveral mislabeled or non-gender pairs turned up in a random sample of 12.
